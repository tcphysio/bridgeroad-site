Cricket

Cricket physio, from someone who has worked in the game

Hamstring and side strains, bowling-related back pain, shoulder problems, and the niggles that build across a long season. Assessed and managed by a physiotherapist with more than 15 years in elite sport, including professional and international cricket.

Common cricket injuries and problems

  • Lower back pain and bone stress in fast bowlers carrying a heavy spell load.
  • Hamstring and side strains, common in bowlers and fielders.
  • Shoulder and rotator cuff problems from bowling, throwing and overhead batting.
  • Knee, ankle and foot pain from repeated front-foot impact and sprints between wickets.
  • Niggles that build across a season rather than appearing in a single moment.

Why cricket assessment is different

Bowling spells, throwing, batting and repeated sprints between wickets place specific, repeatable demands on the body. Workload and physical capacity contribute to many cricket injuries. Assessment looks at the injury itself as well as bowling, throwing, batting, running and training demands.

That means the questions are different. How many overs, across how many days? What changed in the last month? What does your role actually ask of you? A plan built without those answers tends not to hold up across a season.

What is assessed

  • Injury history and current symptoms.
  • Bowling, batting or fielding workload, and any recent spikes.
  • Strength, movement and role-specific capacity.
  • Return-to-play criteria where needed.

Cricket experience

More than 15 years in elite sport, including roles across state, franchise and international cricket: Cricket Victoria, the Melbourne Stars and Melbourne Renegades, Hampshire Cricket, Sri Lanka Cricket and the Bangladesh Cricket Board, alongside AFL environments.

That is the reason return-to-play decisions here are based on objective testing rather than time off, and why rehabilitation is built around bowling spells, batting load and sprint demands rather than a generic timeline.

Selected professional experience

How rehabilitation works

  1. Diagnose. Work out what is driving the problem, and what in your workload contributed to it.
  2. Settle. Calm the irritable phase where that is needed, without stopping everything unnecessarily.
  3. Build. Rebuild the specific capacity your role demands, measured and re-tested as you go.
  4. Return. Graded return to bowling, batting or fielding against criteria, not a date on a calendar.
