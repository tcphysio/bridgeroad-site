Services

Physiotherapy services in Richmond

Nearly everyone walks in because something hurts and it has started costing them. A knee on the stairs. A back at work. A shoulder that has gone at the gym. The job is the same every time: find out what is actually driving it, tell you straight, then build you back to the thing you stopped doing.

Sports & everyday injuries

The front door of the clinic, and where most people land. You do not need to play sport to book here, despite the heading.

What comes through the door. Backs, necks, shoulders, knees, ankles. Muscle strains and tendon pain. The ache that started at a desk and never left. Sports injuries from anything played on a weekend.

How it goes. Diagnosis first, always. Calm it down if it is angry, then make you stronger at the thing you want back. Stairs and a decent night’s sleep for one person, a return to competition for the next. Same principle, different finish line.

What to expect
A diagnosis you can explain to someone else, and something to start on the day.
Who it suits
Anyone sore enough to be reading this page.

Cricket injuries

Cricket injuries, assessed by someone who has spent fifteen years inside the professional game.

What comes through the door. Hamstrings and side strains. Bowlers’ backs, including bone stress. Shoulders worn down by throwing. And the niggles that pile up from October to March until something finally gives.

How it goes. I look at the injury and at your workload, because in cricket the second one usually explains the first. Then we rebuild what your role actually asks of you.

What to expect
A plan that counts your overs, and criteria to meet before you bowl at full tilt.
Who it suits
Club and social cricketers, juniors stepping up a grade, and anyone whose old injury keeps coming back.

Gym & lifting injuries

For people who lift, and have no intention of stopping.

What comes through the door. Shoulders, lower backs and knees that have flared under load. Usually after a technique change, a new program, or a jump in volume that seemed reasonable at the time.

How it goes. I assess the movement, not only the sore bit. Then we change the load rather than pull the plug, wherever that is safe. “Rest for six weeks” is advice for someone who has run out of ideas. You should be able to keep training while this settles, and the clinic has a gym for the part of the fix that needs real weight.

What to expect
A modified program. You keep lifting.
Who it suits
Lifters who would rather adjust than stop.

Running injuries

From a first 5k to a marathon block gone wrong.

What comes through the door. Shins, knees, calves, Achilles and hips. Nearly always a load problem wearing a different hat.

How it goes. I go through what you have been running and what your body is currently good for, because the gap between those two is usually the whole story. Then we manage the load and build the tissue up to the job.

What to expect
A week-by-week return-to-running plan you will actually stick to.
Who it suits
Runners at any distance, including the ones who insist they are not runners.

Return to sport and performance

Feeling better and being ready are two different things, and the space between them is where most re-injuries happen. This is the late stage of rehab, not a separate service.

How it goes. Measured loading and proper testing, so you are cleared on what you hit rather than how many weeks have gone by. A date on the calendar is a guess dressed up as a plan.

What to expect
Numbers to hit, and a staged return based on hitting them.
Who it suits
Anyone coming back from a serious injury, or heading into a pre-season.
