Services

Physiotherapy for everyday life, exercise and sport

Written by Thihan Chandramohan, Physiotherapist. Last reviewed 23 September 2026.

One-to-one physiotherapy in Richmond for pain and injury that is getting in the way of work, training or sport. Every plan starts the same way: a proper assessment, a plain explanation and a first step you understand.

Sports and everyday injuries

Back and neck pain, sore shoulders and knees, strains and tendon pain. You do not need to play sport to book.

This covers the problems seen in most physiotherapy clinics, including symptoms linked with work or daily activity. I assess what is sore, what has changed and what the problem is stopping you from doing. Treatment then settles symptoms where needed and rebuilds the movement, strength or tolerance your goal requires.

What the first appointment involves

An initial consultation is 45 minutes. We go through what happened and what makes it better or worse. I examine the area, explain what I think is going on and how sure I am, and we agree the first steps. Treatment starts on the day where it is appropriate.

What you leave with
An explanation, a working plan and a clear first step.
Who it suits
Richmond locals, active adults and anyone whose pain has begun to limit normal life.

Cricket injuries

Assessment and rehabilitation for bowlers, batters and fielders, with your role and cricket workload built into the plan.

Hamstring and side strains, bowlers’ backs, shoulder problems and the smaller issues that build across a season. The cricket page covers what I assess, how rehabilitation is staged and my background in state, franchise and international cricket.

What you leave with
A cricket-specific plan, sensible workload guidance and clear return criteria.
Who it suits
Junior, social and club cricketers, and players managing recurring or complex injuries.

Gym and lifting injuries

Shoulder, back and knee pain that shows up under load. The aim is to keep you training where it is safe, not to stop by default.

Symptoms often appear after a change in exercise, technique, volume or intensity. I assess the painful movement, your current program and the load you have been managing. Where it is safe, we modify training so you keep doing useful work while rebuilding capacity.

What the first appointment involves

An initial consultation is 45 minutes. Bring your current program and wear gym clothes. Being inside Uplift Gym means we test the lifts involved on the gym floor, not only on a treatment table.

What you leave with
A practical training modification and a route back to normal lifting.
Who it suits
Gym members, strength trainees and anyone whose symptoms appear under load.

Common questions

Running injuries

Shin, knee, calf, Achilles and hip pain from running, assessed against your recent training. You leave with a staged plan back to the distances you want.

Running problems often reflect a mismatch between recent training and what the body is ready to tolerate. We review your symptoms, training history, recent changes and relevant strength or movement findings. From there, I set out what to reduce, what to keep and how to build running again.

What the first appointment involves

An initial consultation is 45 minutes. Bring the shoes you run in most and any record of recent training, such as a watch or app summary. Wear clothes you can run in. The gym’s indoor turf track gives us space to watch you move.

What you leave with
A staged return-to-running plan with useful weekly targets.
Who it suits
New runners, regular recreational runners and competitive athletes across all distances.

Common questions

Return to sport and performance

Late-stage rehabilitation for the gap between feeling better and being ready for full training or competition. Decisions rest on testing, not the calendar alone.

Pain settling is useful, but it does not always mean you are ready. I use relevant strength, power, movement and sport-specific testing to guide progression. The measures depend on the injury and the sport. Time since injury matters, but it is not the only test.

What the appointment involves

The Return to Performance Assessment is 60 minutes. Testing is chosen around your injury and sport. You receive a summary of where you are now, the gaps that remain and the next progression.

What you leave with
Clear measures, remaining priorities and a staged return plan.
Who it suits
Athletes returning from a significant injury, surgery or a long period away from full training.

Common questions

It depends on the injury, your sport and how you perform on testing. Time since injury is one factor. Strength, movement and sport-specific measures guide the decision, and we agree the criteria before you start.
